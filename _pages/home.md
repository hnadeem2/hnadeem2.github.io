I am a computational scientist at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I am a final-year Ph.D. student in Bioengineering (expected Spring 2027). I develop machine-learning models, numerical simulations, and high-performance computing workflows for high-dimensional scientific problems. Across application areas, the same principles apply: represent a complex system, explore it efficiently, and rank options under competing constraints.

My current research is in the [Shukla group](https://shuklagroup.org/), where I study protein conformational ensembles as determinants of function, combining machine learning and molecular simulation to connect sequence, structure, dynamics, and function. I develop adaptive sampling methods, sequence-guided generative models, and machine-learning analyses of large-scale peptide sequence–function data, and I maintain the resulting open-source tools ([Pi-Ensemble, AdaptivePy, and EASE](/software/)). I run GPU-accelerated workflows on HPC systems and collaborate with experimental groups on peptide screening and single-molecule measurements. This line of work has produced 9 papers and preprints (6 first or co-first author), including recent publications in J. Chem. Theory Comput., ACS Chem. Biol., and J. Phys. Chem. Lett.

Before this I worked as a nuclear engineer, including graduate research on computational reactor-core design and several years as a scientist at the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission. Additional detail is on the [Nuclear Engineering](/nuclear/) page.
